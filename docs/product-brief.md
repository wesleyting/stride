# Product Brief v1

## Problem

People often have multiple hobbies, goals, and areas they want to improve, but their progress becomes scattered or forgotten over time. When they return to something after a break, they may not remember where they left off, what was working, or what still needs attention.

Existing tools like Notes or Notion can store this information, but aren't focused on helping someone easily **continue where they left off**.

## Who It's For

People balancing multiple things they want to improve who may struggle with consistency, lose track of gradual progress, or have difficulty returning to something after stepping away.

The product isn't meant to force constant productivity. It's for people who want to improve intentionally while still being able to take breaks, change priorities, and do things purely for fun.

## Desired Outcome

Make returning to something you care about feel like the **path of least resistance**.

Users should be able to understand:

* Where am I now?
* What was I working on?
* What am I trying to achieve?
* What should I focus on next?

Putting something on the back burner shouldn't mean abandoning it.

## Product Philosophy

**Your past self should make things easier for your present self.**

The product should preserve useful context without requiring users to constantly reread old entries or maintain a complicated organizational system.

Progress should be recognized without relying on streaks, badges, or forced encouragement.

## Core Principles

**Low effort**
Recording something should never feel like another task to maintain.

**Easy to return**
A user should be able to leave something for days, weeks, or months and quickly understand where they left off.

**Structured, but flexible**
Different areas of life may need different ways of recording progress.

**Useful over exhaustive**
Users shouldn't need to reread everything they've ever written to benefit from recording it.

**Adaptive**
The product should help surface relevant questions and information based on what is useful to the individual.

**Simple**
It should never become messier or more difficult to manage than the tools it is trying to replace.

## Success

If the product works, users should have a clearer understanding of what they're actively trying to improve, what they're working toward, and what deserves their attention next.

They should be able to intentionally set something aside and return later without feeling like they're starting over.

> **The user maintains their life, not the app.**
